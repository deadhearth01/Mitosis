How to keep work and personal apps separate on one Mac

One Mac for work and personal life is convenient until accounts start mixing: the wrong Slack pings you at dinner, a personal Notion page lands in the work workspace, or you post from the wrong account.

Giving work its own copies of the apps you use keeps the two apart without a second computer or a second macOS user.

Quick answer

Make a work copy of each app you use for both, like Slack (Work), Chrome (Work), and Notion (Work), with Mitosis. Each copy has its own login, data, notifications, and Dock icon.

A simple setup

Pick a badge for work. Use the same letter or color for every work copy, for example a blue W, so work apps are easy to spot in the Dock and in notifications.

Keep work copies together. Put them side by side in the Dock, or in their own Dock folder from ~/Applications/Mitosis.

Quiet hours. Because each copy is its own app, macOS Focus can silence work copies after hours while personal apps keep working.

Step by step

  1. Install Mitosis

    Paste this in Terminal:curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/deadhearth01/Mitosis/main/scripts/install.sh | bashOr use Homebrew: brew install --cask deadhearth01/tap/mitosis. Free; macOS 15 or later on Apple silicon.

  2. Make work copies

    For each app, click New Clone, choose the app, and add the label Work.

  3. Sign in

    Sign in to your work accounts in the copies; your personal apps stay as they are.

  4. Set up Focus

    In System Settings › Focus, choose which apps can notify you; the work copies appear as separate apps.

Good to know

  • Copies update automatically when the original apps update.
  • Turn on Send sign-in links to the right copy for apps that sign in through the browser.

Questions

Is this better than a second macOS user account?

A second user keeps everything apart, but you have to switch users to see it. Copies run side by side in one session, so you can glance at work Slack without logging out of anything.

Does my employer see my personal apps?

Mitosis only keeps app data separate on your Mac. It doesn't change what your company's device management can see.
